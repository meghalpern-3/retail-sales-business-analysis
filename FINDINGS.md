
# Retail Sales Findings

I used SQL to look at sales trends, product performance, and profitability across a simulated retail dataset with 1,200 orders from 2025.

## Which product categories performed best?

Electronics brought in the most revenue at $30,014.79 and had the highest total gross profit at $15,714.79.

What I found interesting was that Office had the highest gross profit margin at 64.25%, even though it generated the least revenue of the four categories. Electronics had a gross margin of 52.36%.

This showed me why it is useful to compare revenue and profit margins instead of looking at sales alone.

## How did sales change throughout the year?

October had the highest revenue at $7,224.72, while April had the lowest at $4,373.89.

One of the biggest changes happened between March and April, when revenue dropped by 28.54%. There were 104 orders in March compared to 78 in April. Average order value also decreased, but by a much smaller amount.

October and November were interesting to compare because both months had 122 orders. Even with the same number of orders, November generated less revenue because average order value fell from $59.22 to $53.25.

I think this is a good example of why looking at order volume and average order value together gives a better picture of sales performance.

## Which regions performed best?

The West generated the most revenue at $18,782.05, followed by the Northeast at $18,031.79.

The Midwest had the lowest revenue at $16,660.15, although the difference between regions was relatively small.

I would want to look at customer counts and regional costs before making any major decisions based on these numbers.

## What I would recommend

1. Look more closely at the Office category. It had the highest gross margin at 64.25%, so I would want to understand whether there are opportunities to increase sales without losing that margin.

2. Investigate the April sales decline. Revenue dropped by 28.54% from March, and most of the change was associated with fewer orders. I would want more information about customer activity, promotions, and inventory before deciding what caused it.

3. Pay attention to average order value alongside order volume. October and November had the same number of orders, but November brought in less revenue. Tracking both metrics could help identify changes that order counts alone might miss.

## Limitations

This project uses simulated data, so these findings are meant to demonstrate an analytical approach rather than describe an actual business.

The gross profit calculations do not include overhead, shipping, taxes, or returns. I also don't have customer acquisition costs or inventory information, which would be important for making more complete business recommendations.
